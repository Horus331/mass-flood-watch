About

Which roads will a flash flood close?

Mass Flood Watch is PhD research at Worcester Polytechnic Institute. It forecasts which road segments in central Massachusetts a flash flood is likely to close, and who would lose access to a hospital, fire station or police station as a result.

The idea

Most flood warnings describe an area: a county, a river, a zone on a map. Road crews and emergency managers need something narrower: which stretches of road will have water on them, when, and what that does to the rest of the network.

The research treats this as a cascade. Heavy rain becomes runoff, runoff becomes water on roads and behind culverts, flooded roads close, and closures cut people off from emergency services. A physics flood model driven by radar and forecast rain follows that chain one step at a time, and every step is checked against what really happened in past storms.

  1. Heavy rain
  2. Runoff and flooding
  3. Water on roads and culverts
  4. Road closures
  5. Loss of access to help
  6. Groundwater and structures (next)

The goal is a framework that connects the hazard, the mechanism and the infrastructure response to a probability of failure and an early warning, starting with roads and extending to culverts, embankments and foundations.

Status

A research prototype under active development. It is not an official warning service and is not affiliated with any state or federal agency.

For emergencies call 911. For official flood warnings, see the National Weather Service, Boston/Norton.

Data and software

Everything the model reads is public. Sources are credited here and linked from each part of How it works.

  • RainNOAA MRMS radar and gauges; NOAA HRRR forecasts
  • Ground heightUSGS 3D Elevation Program (lidar)
  • StreamsUSGS NHDPlus High Resolution
  • SoilsUSDA NRCS SSURGO
  • Land coverMRLC National Land Cover Database 2021
  • CulvertsNorth Atlantic Aquatic Connectivity Collaborative (NAACC); FHWA HDS-5
  • RoadsMassDOT Road Inventory (public data)
  • People and servicesU.S. Census 2020; OpenStreetMap contributors
  • River gauges and imageryUSGS water data; USGS NAIP imagery
  • Flood engineLISFLOOD-FP 8.2, University of Bristol
  • Map and 3DMapLibre GL, three.js; basemap © OpenStreetMap contributors, OpenFreeMap, Esri
