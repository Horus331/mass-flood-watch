Which roads a flash flood will close, when, and why

Mass Flood Watch turns radar rainfall into a road-by-road forecast of flash-flood closures for MassDOT Highway District 3 in central Massachusetts: where water reaches the road, when each segment closes and reopens, and who loses access to a hospital, fire station or police station while it is shut.

How it works

Rain goes in; closed roads and what they cost come out. Every step uses public data and open-source code.

  1. Rain

    NOAA MRMS radar-and-gauge rainfall, 1 km, every hour of the storm.

  2. Flood model

    LISFLOOD-FP 8.2 spreads the rain over USGS 3DEP lidar terrain at 20 m. Soils and land cover set how much soaks in. Validated runs use one converged solver setting for every storm.

  3. Water on every road

    Depth on each road segment every 5 to 30 minutes: when water arrives, how deep, and for how long.

  4. Closure rules

    30 cm of water stops an ordinary car, so the chance of closing rises steeply past 30 cm. A stream reaching road level marks a road that may close. Culverts are checked against FHWA HDS-5.

  5. Network impact

    Residents from the 2020 Census who lose a hospital within 30 min, a fire station within 8 min or police within 10 min; detour delay; risk class and repair cost.

Every run is scored against what happened: USGS river gauges, NWS storm reports, NOAA Storm Events, and documented city and MassDOT closure and damage sites, each compared with random road points.

Validation

Every storm is scored against what happened on the ground, with the same closure rule and the same settings. The one runoff setting (curve number × 0.88) was chosen on Leominster, Sept 2023, from the river gauges alone, then frozen. Leominster and the Worcester, Aug 2021 blind test are development storms. The 23 held-out storms (7 from the storm catalogue and 16 added later from news and agency reports) were each run once without any change.

Forecast, learned model and other tests

  • Forecast skill

    The live forecast runs an 8-member time-lagged ensemble of NOAA HRRR rain forecasts and flags a road when at least 2 of 8 members close it. Issued 9 to 10 h ahead, it catches 32% of Leominster and 58% of Worcester sites, where the single newest forecast caught 8% and 0%. On 7 out-of-sample storms it beat the single forecast in 7 of 14 storm-lead cases and never lost a site; pooled recall rose from 0.21 to 0.34 at long lead.

    Ensemble test · Forecast hindcast. Tested at the earlier curve-number setting (× 0.91); since 25 Oct 2026 the live forecast runs the clean baseline (× 0.88, converged 1 s time step).

  • Learned ranking, 25 storms

    A two-tier candidate (the alert rule, then a learned model filling a 3% budget) caught 26 of 90 held-out sites (0.29) against 15 of 90 for depth alone. It failed the pre-registered significance test after 5 comparisons (P = 0.014 against the 0.01 required), so it is not deployed. In development

    Pre-registered held-out test

  • Tested and not adopted

    Storm drains as a sink: the Leominster pipe network cut city flags by about a third but removed documented sites in proportion. 5-minute rain: peak depths moved about 1 cm; no extra site on any of 7 storms. Connected pavement runoff: still 1 of 15 sites on the four flash-miss storms. Dam storage: more roads flagged, few or no extra sites, so lift fell.

Limitations

  • Light storms are under-predicted. The runoff setting tuned on Leominster leaves summer thunderstorms too dry: the alert rule catches 1 of 58 sites on the 16 newer storms. Drains, 5-minute rain and connected-pavement runoff were each tested and none fixed it.
  • Spatial skill outside the development storms is at chance. On held-out storms the model ranks roads better than chance but does not place the flooded area (FSS at 300 m: 0.019 against 0.234 on the development storms).
  • Labels follow who reported. Outside Leominster, closure sites come from news, NWS and agency reports, which favour named main roads and towns that report. An unreported road is not proof it stayed open, so a flagged road with no report is not necessarily a false alarm, and storms with 2 to 7 sites carry wide uncertainty.
  • 20 m grid. Curbs, gutters and narrow ditches are smaller than one cell, so water on city streets is smoothed out.
  • River floods need basin-scale runs. Large rivers drain land outside the model and peak after the 48-hour window (see the gauge table).
  • Forecast tested on few sites. The ensemble’s out-of-sample test rests on 29 sites across 7 storms, so one site moves a storm’s recall by 0.2 to 0.5.

Sources: clean baseline and recalibration (runs in proto/parity/calib/leo48_cn088/ and proto/parity/catalog/, CPU, 20 m, one converged solver setting), 25-storm scoring and learned-ranking test; gauge and report scores in each run’s score.json and points.json. These replace the earlier GPU runs, which had solver start-up artefacts at the gauges.

What comes next

The parts of the dissertation still being built. Each will appear here and in the storm replay when it is finished and checked against observed storms.

  • In development

    Live forecast

    The next 48 hours of road closures across District 3, from an 8-member time-lagged ensemble of NOAA HRRR rain forecasts.

    NOAA HRRR, the flood model and closure rules above
  • In development

    Learned closure model

    A machine-learning model trained on 25 District 3 storms that turns the flood model’s water, rain and road features into a calibrated chance of closing.

    Documented closures from city, MassDOT and NWS records
  • In development

    Live traffic impact

    Detours and delay measured from live travel times instead of daily counts, so the impact of each closure reflects real congestion.

    MassDOT GoTime travel times
  • In development

    Storm-drain capacity

    The Leominster pipe network is in the model as an opt-in layer; as built it drains too much. Next: inlet clogging and returning the water at the outfalls.

    City of Leominster storm-drain network
  • In development

    Infrastructure damage and repair cost

    Which culverts and road embankments fail, not only which are overtopped: washout, scour and blockage, with the repair bill for each.

    NAACC culvert surveys, FHWA HDS-5, National Bridge Inventory
  • In development

    Cascading hazards

    What the flood leaves behind: groundwater rise after the storm and the risk it poses to foundations, pavements and buried pipes.

    USGS groundwater wells, soils and geotechnical records
