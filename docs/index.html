Which roads a flash flood will close, when, and why

Mass Flood Watch turns radar rainfall into a road-by-road forecast of flash-flood closures for MassDOT Highway District 3 in central Massachusetts: where water reaches the road, when each segment closes and reopens, and who loses access to a hospital, fire station or police station while it is shut.

How it works

Rain goes in; closed roads and what they cost come out. Every step uses public data and open-source code.

  1. Rain

    NOAA MRMS radar-and-gauge rainfall, 1 km, every hour of the storm.

  2. Flood model

    LISFLOOD-FP 8.2 spreads the rain over USGS 3DEP lidar terrain at 20 m. Soils and land cover set how much soaks in. Runs on a GPU on WPI’s Turing cluster.

  3. Water on every road

    Depth on each road segment every 30 minutes: when water arrives, how deep, and for how long.

  4. Closure rules

    30 cm of water stops an ordinary car, so the chance of closing rises steeply past 30 cm. A stream reaching road level marks a road that may close. Culverts are checked against FHWA HDS-5.

  5. Network impact

    Residents from the 2020 Census who lose a hospital within 30 min, a fire station within 8 min or police within 10 min; detour delay; risk class and repair cost.

Every run is scored against what happened: USGS river gauges, NWS storm reports, NOAA Storm Events, and documented city and MassDOT closure and damage sites, each compared with random road points.

Known limits

  • 20 m grid. Curbs, gutters and narrow ditches are smaller than one cell, so water on city streets is smoothed out.
  • Storm drains and small culverts are not resolved yet. The model drains no more than 2 mm of rain per hour through pipes, so some low spots stay flooded longer than they did.
  • Short urban downpours are under-predicted. In the Worcester 2021 storm only 56% of reports had 30 cm of modelled water nearby.
  • River floods need basin-scale runs. Large rivers drain land outside the model box and keep rising after the 48-hour window; 5 of the 10 District 3 gauges show no modelled rise, hence the 22 h timing error.

What comes next

The parts of the dissertation still being built. Each will appear here and in the storm replay when it is finished and checked against observed storms.

  • In development

    Live forecast

    The next 48 hours of road closures across District 3, rerun every six hours from the NOAA HRRR rain forecast on WPI’s Turing GPU cluster.

    NOAA HRRR, the flood model and closure rules above
  • In development

    Learned closure model

    A machine-learning model trained on nine District 3 storms that turns the flood model’s water, rain and road features into a calibrated chance of closing.

    Documented closures from city, MassDOT and NWS records
  • In development

    Live traffic impact

    Detours and delay measured from live travel times instead of daily counts, so the impact of each closure reflects real congestion.

    MassDOT GoTime travel times
  • In development

    Storm-drain capacity

    The city pipe network drawn into the flood model, so streets drain as fast as their pipes allow instead of a single district-wide rate.

    City of Leominster storm-drain network
  • In development

    Infrastructure damage and repair cost

    Which culverts and road embankments fail, not only which are overtopped: washout, scour and blockage, with the repair bill for each.

    NAACC culvert surveys, FHWA HDS-5, National Bridge Inventory
  • In development

    Cascading hazards

    What the flood leaves behind: groundwater rise after the storm and the risk it poses to foundations, pavements and buried pipes.

    USGS groundwater wells, soils and geotechnical records

Data

Rain: NOAA MRMS. Terrain and river gauges: USGS 3DEP and NWIS. Roads, traffic counts and repeat flood sites: MassGIS and MassDOT. Culverts: North Atlantic Aquatic Connectivity Collaborative (NAACC). Bridges: FHWA National Bridge Inventory. Residents: US Census 2020. Hospitals, fire and police stations: © OpenStreetMap contributors. Storm reports: NOAA National Weather Service. Flood model: LISFLOOD-FP 8.2, University of Bristol. Basemaps: CARTO and Esri, © OpenStreetMap contributors.
