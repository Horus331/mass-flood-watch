How the model works

Mass Flood Watch · a free, open, physics-based flood engine for Massachusetts. Main map · v2 map · methods · work log

1. What it computes

For a storm (past or forecast) the engine computes how deep the water gets on every 30 m × 30 m cell of the state, every 30 minutes, from the rain that fell, the shape of the ground, how fast the soil can drink, how much of the surface is paved, and how much water the big rivers carry in from New Hampshire and Vermont. It then reads the water depth on each of the state's 52,698 road segments and traces which dry roads get cut off or carry detour traffic.

3. Physics

The water moves with the 2-D local-inertial shallow-water equations (Bates, Horritt & Fewtrell 2010, the scheme used by LISFLOOD-FP), solved on the GPU with CuPy: for each cell face the flow is updated from the water-surface slope and friction (Manning n = 0.06 on land), and each cell's depth from the net inflow. The time step follows the CFL limit (≤ 10 s). Water that reaches the sea leaves the model. The GPU and CPU versions of the solver are checked against each other on every run (max difference 1.1 × 10-4 m).

Rain that lands on a cell first infiltrates following Horton's curve f = fc + (f0 − fc)·exp(−F / k), where the cumulative infiltration F starts at the storm's antecedent wetness (120 mm for the saturated December 2023 ground, 0 for dry September 2023). f0 and fc come from the soil group; the paved fraction scales infiltration down and storm drains remove up to 2 + 5·(paved) mm/h.

4. Why this is not just a flood map

Each run produces water depth on every road segment at every time step. From that the site traces the knock-on effects: dry roads that lose every path to a numbered route (cut off) and the neighbours that receive detour traffic (60 % to the next road, 40 % two roads away, weighted by traffic counts). This is the first link of the cascade the project is about: rain → water on the ground → roads and culverts → the network around them.

5. Validation (in progress)

Each published run is scored with sim/validate.py: modelled peak depth at every surveyed high-water mark of the same storm versus the surveyed height above ground (bias, mean absolute error, share within 50 cm), and the hit rate at dated road-flooding reports against the hit rate at random road points. Results are published next to each run as validation.json.

Known limits: 30 m cells hide culverts and small channels; rain inside the grid is counted once but the Deerfield inflow may overlap its in-state basin; no groundwater table dynamics yet; tide and surge enter only through gauge levels at the coast.

6. Software

Everything is in the open repository Horus331/mass-flood-watch. Data preparation runs on GitHub Actions, the flood engine on Google Colab (NVIDIA L4), the results live on Hugging Face (mass-flood-watch-tiles) as map tiles, and the site is served by GitHub Pages with MapLibre GL and OpenFreeMap. There is no server and no paid service anywhere in the chain.
