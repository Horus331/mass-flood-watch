# State of the art — fast flood engines, ML surrogates, infrastructure failure, competitors (as of 19 Oct 2026)

Research-scout pass for mass-flood-watch. Scope: what exists now (priority 2024–2026), what each does better than us, and what we do that it does not.
Our reference point (engine v3, commit `67669294`): GPU 2-D local-inertial (Bates 2010 / de Almeida & Bates 2013 q-centred θ = 0.7, Fr ≤ 2) on 10–30 m grids
block-averaged from 3DEP 10 m, MRMS Pass-2 hourly rain, sub-grid channels (simplified Neal 2012) from NHDPlus HR + Bieger 2015 bankfull geometry,
Horton + storage-limited infiltration from SSURGO with a 14-day MRMS antecedent store, paved-only drains, tide boundary, USGS inflows; validated on USGS STN HWMs
(WSE at the deepest cell within 60 m) and 4,551 MassDOT road-flood reports under a pre-registered protocol; L2 culvert/crossing exceedance from the
MA Stream Crossings service; GBM surrogate table + planned GNN.

### What the theory says about our engine on steep reaches
Cozzolino et al. 2019 ([arXiv 1904.12564](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12564)) [paper] collect the known limits of the local-inertia approximation (LInA): adequate for Fr < ~0.5 and for 0.5–1 only with mild depth gradients (de Almeida & Bates 2013); fails for supercritical flow (Martins et al. 2016); flood extent underestimated above Fr ≈ 0.8 and calibrated roughness differs from full-SWE values (Cea & Bladé 2015); energy is not conserved across bed discontinuities and solutions may not exist for drops comparable to the flow depth. Implication for us: on 10–30 m cells over 2–5 % slopes the bed drops 0.2–1.5 m per cell, i.e. exactly the "discontinuous bed" regime; any roughness we calibrate will be **effective** (compensating numerics), not physical, and should not be transferred to a full-SWE model. The EGU25 abstract by an Italian group proposes adding LES turbulence to overland flow for steep natural watersheds ([EGU25-13519](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU25/EGU25-13519.html)) [abstract].

On structures: the USGS Lidar Base Specification says **bridges are removed** from bare-earth DEMs but **culverts are retained** (road fill left intact over the culvert; uncertain structures are treated as culverts), and only streams ≥ 30 m wide are hydro-flattened ([USGS spec](https://www.usgs.gov/ngp-standards-and-specifications/lidar-base-specification-digital-elevation-model-surface)). So every culvert in the 3DEP DEM is a **digital dam** unless we breach it. Chen, Tong & Liang (Loughborough, [EGU26-22261](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-22261.html)) [abstract] automate detection of such "flow-permeable structures" from water-surface + river-network data for England's 2 m DTM and find unresolved structures dominate urban inundation error while missing bathymetry dominates channel-hydraulics error. A French benchmark (Godet et al. 2025, ESSD 17, 2963) [paper] shows the size of the bathymetry effect: median water-level error vs 171 rating curves **+0.54 m with the raw 5 m DTM vs −0.04 m with a streambed-corrected DTM** (2-yr flood); event errors vs 2,287 HWMs −0.2 to −0.6 m ([ESSD](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/2963/2025/essd-17-2963-2025.html)). Our +2 m high bias is far outside this envelope → look for a structural cause, not just tuning.

### Engines — better than us / we do that they don't
- **Better than us:** HEC-RAS 2D and TUFLOW HPC represent culverts/bridges hydraulically and solve full SWE (correct supercritical flow, jumps) — precisely what our steep reaches need. SFINCS sub-grid tables give fine-grid accuracy at 35–50× lower cost; TUFLOW SGS does the same per face. RIM2D shows multi-GPU scaling and real-time operation for a whole city with honest citizen-observation validation. LISFLOOD-FP 8.1 has adaptive non-uniform GPU grids. NWS FIM is now an operational-ish, free baseline over all of MA.
- **We do that they don't:** a single open GPU model of a whole state at 10–30 m driven by observed hourly radar rain with antecedent soil state and tides, run as a **reproducible queue** of storms; per-reach WSE validation against all STN marks under a **pre-registered protocol**; direct coupling to a road network (52,698 segments sampled every 30 min), crossings capacity (L2) and the road-report ranker. None of the open engines ships an infrastructure-consequence layer.

- **Better than us:** Google has orders of magnitude more labels (Groundsource) and global meteorological forcing; mSWE-GNN shows mesh-general GNN surrogates with a mass-conservation plausibility check we should copy; FIMserv/Flood-LDM show cheap "low-fidelity → high-fidelity" correction, which is exactly a 30 m → 10 m path for us; TabPFN shows a foundation-model baseline that beats tuned GBMs out of distribution.
- **We do that they don't:** physics corpus with per-cell time-to-wet, duration, velocity and h·v (not just peak depth) at 10–30 m over a whole state, tied to an infrastructure graph; pre-registered LOSO + HUC10 splits; validation on surveyed HWMs and road reports rather than only on simulator output; Google's flash-flood product is 20 km — four orders of magnitude coarser in area than a road segment.

### Bottom line for positioning
1. Hazard-mapping vendors (First Street, Fathom, JBA) already sell 5–10 m US flood depth. A PhD contribution cannot be "another statewide depth map"; it has to be **event-specific, validated, and consequence-linked** (roads, crossings, groundwater).
2. The most credible gap: **observed-storm, crossing-aware hydraulics with a calibrated L1 and a computed L2**, and a transparent benchmark dataset — there is no New England equivalent of the French ESSD flash-flood benchmark.
3. Watch UMass (Boakye/Asgari, Deerfield culverts, 2026) and the USGS crossing tool: overlap risk, but also the obvious collaborators/data sources.
