Research

Which roads will a flash flood close, and who gets cut off?

Flood warnings describe whole towns. Road crews need to know which stretches of road will have water on them. This research follows the water from the rain cloud to the road, road by road, across central Massachusetts.

The question

A flood is a chain, not a single event

Heavy rain runs off the ground, fills streams and culverts, and spills onto roads. A closed road then sends traffic and ambulances the long way round. Each link can make the next one worse.

Heavy rain

Short, intense storms on small, steep streams.

Water on the road

Streams overtop culverts; low streets pond.

Closed roads

Deep or fast water makes a road unsafe.

Help takes longer

Detours push hospitals and fire stations out of reach.

How we check it

Tested on storms it has never seen

Rules written first

Every test is written down and dated before the model is run, so results cannot be tuned after the fact.

Unseen storms

The model is set up on one storm and then judged on past storms it was never adjusted to, in central Massachusetts and beyond.

Against what agencies use today

Its road list is compared with flood warnings, flood-zone maps and simpler rules, using real reports of flooded roads.

Against river gauges

Modelled water levels are compared with U.S. Geological Survey stream gauges for every storm.

Where each part stands

The model is built in parts, from the rain to the people who lose access to help. Each part shows how far it is built, what is left, the problem we are working on now, and how far it has been checked against real storms.
